A sub-stepping child is unstable under flux matching

solvedIDEALNEAB2D

Seen: IDEAL E2. The volume was conserved to 2e-16, yet the strait level grew to 23 m within 12 h.

What triggers it: switching parts off one at a time showed the combination of

  • flux matching (the parent's elevation correction next to the interface),
  • a child that sub-steps (time ratio > 1),
  • under a parent stepping well above the child's step.

The growth rises with the parent's Courant number. Two levels are enough, and neither tidal forcing nor friction removes it. A 1D model of the same coupling (IDEAL/scripts/coupling1d.py) reproduces it: growth factors per parent step of 1.013 at Courant 0.57 and 1.12 at 0.9 (time ratio 3).

The mode: it lives in the child, a few cells wide, and changes sign every 1–2 parent steps. Short waves the parent cannot represent are pumped by the loop flux mismatch → correction of the parent cell outside the window → the child's ring.

Fix: an AGRIF-like sponge (Debreu et al. 2012). The child's transports are damped next to the interface through pyGETM's horizontal viscosity (momentum.An, land-aware): NestedBarotropic(sponge_width=, sponge_alpha=). It acts on momentum only, so the volume stays exactly conserved. It is stable for α = 0.03–0.2, default 0.1.

pyGETM applied the transport damping with the time step twice

solvedpyGETMIDEAL

Seen: the sponge became unstable above α = 0.02, far below the explicit limit.

Cause: a unit analysis of src/momentum.F90 (damp) showed that the damping was multiplied by the time step twice. This is also in upstream pyGETM. The 0.25 limit on α is the explicit diffusion limit for the 2Δx mode, and the tendency's units were mislabelled (m⁻² s⁻² instead of m² s⁻²).

Fix: fork commit 77469d3. The sponge is now stable for α = 0.03–0.2.

Subdomains holding only a child's hole got a core

solvedNEABload balancing

Seen: in the NEAB balance figures, the root had cores for subdomains that lie under its children.

Cause: the hole is wet (EXTERNAL), so pyGETM kept those subdomains.

Fix: Domain.tiling_mask and Level.tiling_mask (fork 296f411): only subdomains with cells the parent reads (its rim) stay. The NEAB root now needs 7 cores at 64, and 52–54 instead of about 80 at 512; wall time −6 to −9 %.

One process split differs by 1e-4 m

openIDEAL2D

The results are bit-identical for the process splits 1/1/1, 2/3/2, 1/2/1 and 1/1/2. With the channel on 3 processes its tile edges fall on the shelf window's edges, and the results differ by 1e-4 m. Not yet explained.

Streaks in the animations

solvedIDEAL

Bands and diagonal streaks in the animated elevation were GIF quantisation (a palette per frame, dithered), not the model: the fields have no grid-scale noise. The GIF is now made from the MP4 with one ffmpeg palette.

Exact content in the 3D rim made a uniform field non-uniform

solvedIDEAL3D

Seen: with a uniform temperature, the 3D coupling made it non-uniform by 2e-4 °C in 12 h at the shelf's rim.

Cause: the planned rim form B″ restored each rim column's content exactly in the parent's volume. At the tracer stage the parent's rim still has the elevation of the previous 2D sync, so the child columns' volume differs from the parent cell's, and the exact content then means a different concentration.

Fix: concentrations by the child volume each parent layer receives. A uniform field stays uniform to 3e-13; exact conservation is left to the 3D flux register.

The parent's 3D transport at the interface was 10 % off

solvedIDEAL3DpyGETM

Seen: building the 3D flux register. The water through the window faces over a 3D step differed by about 10 % between parent and child, although the 2D coupling matches them.

Cause: pyGETM sums the 2D transports over the 3D step for the 3D momentum (Ui) in advance_depth_integrated, before the 2D coupler replaces the parent's transports at the window faces.

Fix: NestedBarotropic adds its replacement to that sum as well (fork 02a1eb1). The difference is 1e-4. This affects every nested 3D run.

Parent and child move different water through each layer of the interface

solvedIDEAL3D

Seen: a per-layer tracer correction that conserves the content exactly made a uniform field 15 °C non-uniform.

Cause: the 2D coupling matches only the column total. The child, with more layers, resolves a jet at the pycnocline that the parent does not, so the layer profiles of the water through a face differ strongly. A per-layer tracer correction without the matching water changes concentrations in thin layers.

Fix: at the momentum_3d stage the parent takes the child's layer transports at the window faces, remapped onto its own face layers with its column total kept, and recomputes its vertical velocity before it advects. With the 3D flux register the heat content of a closed basin is then conserved to 1e-9, and a uniform field stays uniform (fork 02a1eb1).

3D runs need the sponge too

solvedIDEAL3D

The first 48 h IDEAL 3D runs went NaN at 11 h, with and without the 3D coupling. The strait level grew from about 7 h: the sub-stepping instability of the 2D coupling (above), which the 3D model had been run without. With the sponge (3 child cells, α 0.1, now the default of ideal3d.py) all runs are stable.

Spurious currents at the interfaces in a lake at rest

solvedIDEAL3Dtest setup

Seen: IDEAL V2, a lake at rest. The nested runs had spurious currents of 1–3 cm/s in a frame along the interfaces, ten times the single model's. They were the same with sigma and with adaptive layers, and vanished without the 3D ring.

Cause: the test setup. Every level sampled the analytic temperature profile at its own layer centres. Across a 3 m thick pycnocline the value at the centre of a 5 m layer is far from the layer's mean, so the parent held a different discretisation of the same state than the child, and its ring gave the child 0.4 °C differences at the pycnocline: a horizontal density gradient at the interface.

Fix: every level starts from the exact layer means of the analytic profile (the tanh integrates in closed form). The column means are then identical for 10, 15 and 20 layers. This alone did not remove the currents; what remains is the ring's vertical reconstruction.

The 3D ring could not carry the child's vertical structure

solvedIDEAL3D

Seen: IDEAL V2 after the consistent initial state: 1–2 cm/s of spurious current along the interfaces in a lake at rest, against about 1 mm/s without the 3D ring.

Cause: the ring's profile comes from the parent's coarser layers, remapped piecewise-constant: a 3 m thick pycnocline in 5 m layers lands in the child's ring as steps of up to 1 °C.

Tried:

  • Keeping every interface at its depth in the remap (only the bottom layer cut or extended): the right map for flat isopycnals over a slope, but no measurable difference here.
  • Adding the child's own vertical detail to the ring: its adjacent interior column, minus the same column through the parent's layers and back. This halves the currents.
  • A limited piecewise-parabolic reconstruction (PPM, Colella and Woodward 1984, for layers of unequal thickness) inside each source layer of the remap, instead of a constant. It keeps the content exactly and adds no new extrema; from 10 onto 20 layers across a 3 m pycnocline its error is 0.11 °C against 0.79 °C.

Result: with PPM (now the default, fork remap_scheme="ppm") the spurious currents drop from 21 to 6.5 mm/s on the shelf and from 8.6 to 2.2 mm/s in the channel (single model 1.4 mm/s), and the pycnocline moves 0.03 m instead of 0.16 m. What is left is largest on the shelf over the slope; a relaxation of the 3D ring values (a sponge for the scalars) is the next thing to try if it matters.

The remap's outer layers were constant

solvedIDEAL3D

Seen: IDEAL V2 (a lake at rest), measured against each level's grid run alone with the same setup: the coupling added 1.4x the single grid's spurious current in the channel, 3.9x on the shelf and 2.1x in the strait. Without the 3D ring the shelf was at the single grid's level. The shelf's maximum sat at its east interface, where the water is 20 m deep and the pycnocline at 15 m lies in the bottom layers.

Cause: even at rest the ring's temperature was 0.010-0.016 °C off the child's own layer means there. The ring is built from the child's adjacent column (20.125 m deep), remapped onto the ring column (20.0 m), and the PPM reconstruction kept the outer two layers at each end constant. A 0.12 m shift of the sigma interfaces inside a constant layer, against a gradient of about 1 °C/m, gives that error.

Fix: the second layer from each end gets a parabola too, its outer edge linear between the two layer means (still no new extrema; numpy and the compiled kernel alike, fork 11c0709). The ring error drops to 0.006 °C, and the spurious currents to 1.0x the single grid in the channel, 1.4x on the shelf and 2.1x in the strait. V5 and V6 are unchanged (pycnocline depth within 1.3 cm rms, the same extrema), heat in a closed basin is conserved to 5e-11 and a uniform field stays uniform to 3e-13. The strait's maximum is one face where the wall meets its west interface: a corner, like the wall's gap that is the single grids' own maximum.

The ring's child-detail correction created new extrema

solvedIDEAL3D

Seen: IDEAL V6. A dye that starts at exactly 0 and 1 reaches 1 + 3.6e-4 on the shelf and −1.2e-4 in the strait.

Cause: the ring's child-detail correction (added for V2) is an addition: the child's interior column minus its round trip through the parent's layers. It is not limited, so near a sharp front it can push a ring value outside the range of its neighbours. Without it the shelf and strait stay within 1e-7. A smaller undershoot of 4e-5 in the channel, the same with and without it, comes from the flux correction on the parent's side.

Fix: the corrected ring value is limited, layer by layer, to the range of the parent's remapped value and the child's adjacent value. The shelf's overshoot drops from 3.6e-4 to 1.2e-5 and the strait's undershoot from 1.2e-4 to 6e-11; the lake at rest (V2) is not worse (shelf 6.5 → 4.9 mm/s). What remains is in the parent, the channel: 1.9e-4 above and 4e-5 below, from the flux correction. The plan's safeguard for it (spreading a correction that would make a new extremum over the column) is not done yet.

The 3D exchange took a third of a run

openIDEAL3Dspeed

Seen: IDEAL V7. With every part of the 3D exchange timed (work and waiting), the exchange in numpy added 31 % to a run with temperature and salinity, and 80 % with 14 tracers; on the shelf it was 21 % and 37 % of the run time.

Cause: about 85 % of its work were the vertical remaps of the rim and the ring, vectorised numpy, growing linearly with the number of tracers (the ring's child-detail correction needs three more remaps per tracer).

Done:

  • A compiled remap kernel in pyGETM (_pygetm.vertical_remap, Fortran: one pass down each column, PCM or PPM). It is 15–16 times faster than numpy and gives the same results to 4e-15. The exchange's work per 3D step fell 2–4 times: on the shelf from 26 to 13 ms (T, S) and from 124 to 36 ms (14 tracers), 9 % and 17 % of the run.
  • Compiled gather and scatter of columns from and into pyGETM's 3D arrays, and messages packed column by column, so that no transposes are left. Less than 10 % more: the transposes were not the cost.
  • The 3D cost in nesting-balance: the rim and ring remaps per model and 3D step, calibrated from IDEAL (exchange_3d_base 0.40–0.54, exchange_3d 0.20–0.23 per tracer, in wet-cell 2D steps; rim and ring estimates within 10 %).

    • Left: the remaining work is spread over many small numpy operations, where Python's cost per call dominates; moving whole steps (the rim's assembly, remap and write; the ring's remap, correction and limiter) into single compiled routines might give another 1.5 times on the exchange, a few per cent of a run. The waiting for partner levels (up to 46 ms per 3D step in the channel) is for the balancer, which now knows the 3D cost: balanced, IDEAL on 12 cores runs 7 % faster and the channel waits a third as long (V7).

    The pure-Python path stays: every compiled routine has its numpy version, used when pyGETM is built without them or with PYGETM_NESTING_REMAP=numpy, so the remap can also be changed and tried in numpy first, with a simpler interface.

    With FABM the shares go down: the exchange grows with the number of tracers, the computation faster (sources and sinks in every cell and layer).

    nesting-balance --calibrate counted the 3D waiting as computation

    solvednesting-balance3Dspeed

    Seen: balancing IDEAL in 3D. Calibrated from a run, the balancer chose the uniform split, which measured slower than its uncalibrated choice; the channel's cost factor came out at twice the others'.

    Cause: the model's own computation in the runs' timing.json still held the 3D exchange, and with it the 3D coupler's waiting for the partner levels (7.8 s of the channel's 18 s).

    Fix: the 3D syncs and transports are subtracted (own_compute); the cost factors are then 0.87–0.99 and the choices those of the model.

    Then: the 2D coupling's own work (the ring values, flux register and restriction on the child; flux matching, transport replacement and slopes on the parent; their halos and messages) calibrated from five IDEAL 2D runs at different splits (--fit-exchange): about 27 per rim cell, 2.4 per ring cell and child step and 900 per message, in wet-cell 2D steps, 11 % rms. A parent's work per interface cell is about ten times a child's. With every term calibrated, the balancer's predictions are within 5–13 % of the measured runs: it finds the large imbalances (12 cores: 2/4/6) but not differences of a few per cent (6 cores).

    nesting-balance gave every IDEAL level split 1

    solvednesting-balance3Dspeed

    Seen: balancing IDEAL with a FABM-like load (32 tracers, four times the computation). The balancer moved cores from the channel and the shelf to the strait (1 / 3 / 8 on 12 cores), and the run was 41 % slower than the uniform split.

    Cause: the balancer read split (2D steps per 3D step) from each level's own block. IDEAL sets it once, for the root, in config:, and all levels share the 3D step, so a child's split is the root's times its 2D steps per root step (10, 10 and 30). Every level got split 1: its 3D cost was 10–30 times too high, the strait's most. With T and S alone the 3D cost was small enough to hide this.

    Fix: a level without its own split gets the root split times its time ratios down the hierarchy. The balancer then chooses 2 / 4 / 6 on 12 cores, which is faster than the uniform split.

    The emulated FABM load fed waiting back into itself

    solvedIDEAL3Dspeed

    Seen: a 24 h IDEAL run with --compute-factor 4 had not reached its first 6 h output after 48 minutes; it should take about four.

    Cause: after every 3D step each process computed for three times the time of its own last step. That time held its waiting on the neighbouring processes of its level, which were themselves computing for longer: the waiting grew from step to step.

    Fix: a fixed amount of work per 3D step, calibrated once on 3D steps 2–6 without the extra load. A biogeochemical model's cost is work per cell, not time, so this is also the better emulation. The run now takes about four minutes.

    DKm runs with the wrong bottom roughness

    solvedDKm

    The first DKm tidal runs used z0 = 0.001 m, not the 0.015 m NEAB uses, which made the Kattegat's tide look twice too large. The runs were repeated with 0.015 m. A z0 map from the seabed substrate was tried for NEAB and does not change the North Sea tide; z0 stays uniform.

    TPXO9 at the coast: wet nodes without constants

    solvedreference data

    Next to the coast TPXO9-atlas has nodes with a depth but zero constants, and interpolating between nodes mixes in land (zero): at Hinkley Point the interpolated M2 was 0. The series now take the nearest node with constants, without interpolation. In the reference maps, only water connected to the Atlantic is kept: inside the box the Adriatic has amplitudes of metres that are not real.

From the Danish North Sea coast into the Skagerrak and the Kattegat it is about twice too large (Skagen 0.29 m against 0.14 m observed). The error is the same at both resolutions, and a bottom roughness map from the seabed substrate does not remove it.

    The amphidrome in front of the German Bight lies about 0.3° south-west of TPXO9's. The region off Jutland and the Skagerrak lies between that amphidrome and the Norwegian one. Its small amplitudes are very sensitive to where the amphidromes are, so a small shift doubles them there. The most likely cause is too little dissipation in the south-eastern North Sea:

    • The Wadden Sea flats are not in the bathymetry: after the regridding they are land or have no depth (the sources refer to the lowest astronomical tide).
    • The models mask water shallower than 5 m.
    • The tide coming in from the Atlantic is also 5–17 % too large (no tidal potential, 12 km cells over the north-western shelf), but that is a smaller effect.

    A Wadden Sea level

    A sixth level, wadden, nests in north_sea_baltic over the Wadden Sea from Den Helder to Skallingen (4.6–9.2°E, 52.9–55.7°N) at ratio 3, about 1.5 km in the coarse setup, with the open North Sea in front of it. Its bathymetry includes the intertidal flats:

    • EMODnet without its land mask: the flats keep their height above the lowest astronomical tide (LAT).
    • Land is taken from the Natural Earth land polygons, not from the sign of the depth, so the polders behind the dikes, below sea level, stay land.
    • The depths are taken from LAT to mean sea level: MSL − LAT is estimated from TPXO9 as the sum of its constituents' amplitudes, about 1.2–1.5 m along the Dutch coast, 2–2.5 m in the German Bight and 1 m at Esbjerg.
    • Water up to 1 m above MSL is kept wet. pyGETM's drying and flooding handles the flats.

    Most of the flats then lie 0–2 m below MSL: 3,800 km² that fall dry at the lowest tide are water in this level, where north_sea_baltic has land.

    The flats lower the Skagerrak and off Jutland by 15–20 %, and the Kattegat gauges with them, more than any of the sensitivity runs above. But they also take 15–25 % of the tide in the German Bight and the Wadden Sea itself, which was about right or too small before. With z0 0.015 m the drag on water less than a metre deep is several times that in 20 m, so the flats likely dissipate too much. A second run with z0 0.002 m in the Wadden Sea level only (north_sea_baltic keeps 0.015 m) helps on both sides. The Skagerrak comes down further, to 1.81 times TPXO9, and Skagen to 0.22 m. In the Wadden Sea about half of the lost tide comes back, but it is still lower than in the control run, and the error over all gauges is 0.19 m against 0.17 m. Lower friction also lowering the Skagerrak shows that the flats act through where the tide goes, not only through what they dissipate.

    A third run tests a depth-dependent z0 in the Wadden Sea level: z0 = 0.015 m × min(1, H/10 m), at least 0.0005 m, with H the depth below MSL (--z0-depth 10). pyGETM's drag already uses the actual water depth, so with a constant z0 the drag coefficient on a flat 1 m deep is 0.013, against 0.004 in 20 m. With z0(H) it stays at about 0.005 on the flats, while the channels and the open sea, 86 % of the level's cells, keep 0.015 m. The result is almost that of the constant 0.015 m: the friction on the flats themselves hardly matters. What the 0.002 m run changed was the friction in the channels and in the sea in front of the flats. Even with it, the Wadden Sea level lowers the German Bight by 10–15 % against the control run. That points to the flats themselves, their area and height: MSL − LAT from the amplitude sum is an upper bound, so the flats may lie too deep and take too much water. Checking them against a survey-based bathymetry with heights is the next step, and production runs need a closer look at the bathymetry in any case.

    Two more runs raise the flats: MSL − LAT is taken from the M2 amplitude alone (wadden_bathymetry.py --msl-lat m2), 0.01–1.52 m instead of 0.05–3.02 m. That leaves 1 km³ less water in the part shallower than 5 m, and 140 more cells above MSL. With z0 0.002 m in the Wadden Sea level, the German Bight comes back to 0.93 of TPXO9, and Helgoland, Cuxhaven and Wilhelmshaven to within 10–20 % of the gauges. The Skagerrak, however, drops less (1.98 instead of 1.81 times TPXO9). Over all gauges the two flat heights are equal (0.18 m against 0.19 m), and both are still above the control run's 0.17 m. Higher flats help the Wadden Sea and cost the Skagerrak, so the flats alone do not settle it.

    The depth datum and the depth source

    Two 31-day runs test the North Sea depths themselves, without the Wadden Sea level.

    LAT to MSL. EMODnet gives depths below the lowest astronomical tide (LAT), and GEBCO 2026 is built from it on the shelf, while pyGETM's elevation is relative to mean sea level. So the shelf is too shallow by MSL − LAT. NEAB/scripts/lat_to_msl.py computes MSL − LAT as LAT is defined: the lowest level of a TPXO9 prediction (12 constituents, nodal corrections), hourly over 2015 and 2024, near both ends of the 18.6-year nodal cycle, on a 1/10° grid. That gives about 1.6 m at Den Helder, 2.8 m at Cuxhaven, 2.0 m at Esbjerg, 4.7 m at Dover and 7 m at Saint-Malo. The offset is added to every cell at least 5 m below LAT, so the set of wet cells stays the same. The median change is 0.9 m in north_sea_baltic and 1.4 m in the root.

    The deeper shelf is the larger improvement so far. Over north_sea_baltic's 225 gauges the M2 rms error drops from 0.19 m to 0.15 m, and over all 369 gauges from 0.17 m to 0.15 m. The Southern Bight comes from 0.81 to 0.95 of TPXO9, Hoek van Holland from 0.54 m to 0.70 m (observed 0.78 m), Helgoland from 0.98 m to 1.06 m (1.06 m), and the German Bight and Southern Bight amphidromes move to within 0.1–0.2° of TPXO9's. But the Skagerrak gets worse: 3.2 times TPXO9, Skagen 0.40 m (observed 0.14 m), and the Kattegat gauges with it (danish_straits rms 0.12 m against 0.07 m). The map shows why. The Norwegian amphidrome lies at about 5.3°E 58.6°N, against 6.0°E 58.3°N in TPXO9, so the Skagerrak lies further from it. With the south-eastern amphidromes right, the Skagerrak error is now on the Norwegian side: the depths of the Norwegian Trench and the Skagerrak, which LAT → MSL hardly changes (MSL − LAT is 0.2–0.5 m there), or how the tide enters along the Norwegian coast.

    EMODnet instead of GEBCO. GEBCO 2026 is a patchwork in the North Sea. It is about 0.5 m shallower than EMODnet in the Southern Bight and the German Bight, about the same in the central North Sea, and 2–4 m shallower off Jutland, with straight seams at 0.5°E and 60°N between its sources. NEAB/scripts/swap_depth_source.py adds the difference of the two cell means to north_sea_baltic's depths, in 55,000 of its 101,000 wet cells, keeping the mask and the fixes. The result is almost the same as the control run: rms 0.173 m over all gauges, the Southern Bight 0.82 of TPXO9. Only the Skagerrak goes up, from 2.40 to 2.79 times TPXO9, Skagen from 0.29 m to 0.34 m. The deeper water off Jutland moves the tide the wrong way there.

    Both together. The flats lowered the Skagerrak but took tide from the German Bight, and the deeper shelf does the opposite, so a third run combines them: all levels from LAT to MSL, plus the Wadden Sea level with its flats (from M2, z0 0.002 m). The two effects roughly add up. Against the LAT → MSL run, the Skagerrak comes down from 3.24 to 2.45 times TPXO9, back to the control run's level, and Skagen from 0.40 m to 0.30 m. The Kattegat gauges are as good as in the control run (danish_straits rms 0.08 m), and the Norwegian amphidrome moves to about 5.6°E 58.5°N, closer to TPXO9's. The Southern Bight keeps its gain (0.94). But the Wadden Sea loses tide again: List 0.65 m (observed 0.78 m), Cuxhaven Mittelgrund 1.18 m (1.37 m), Eemshaven 1.00 m (1.15 m). Over the North Sea gauges the rms is 0.16 m against 0.15 m without the flats, and 0.153 m against 0.149 m over all gauges. The flats in this run are still those made for the old datum, with MSL − LAT from M2 alone.

    One datum for all levels. A last run builds the flats with the same MSL − LAT as the other levels (wadden_bathymetry.py --offset, the lowest level of the two-year TPXO9 prediction). In the inner Wadden Sea that is much larger than before: 1.6 m at Harlingen, 1.9 m at Delfzijl and 2.7 m at Wilhelmshaven, against 0.1–0.9 m from the amplitude sum carried in from open water. So the flats there lie lower. This gives the best Skagerrak and Kattegat of all runs: the Skagerrak at 1.96 times TPXO9, off Jutland 1.37, Skagen 0.24 m, and the Kattegat gauges at an rms of 0.056 m, against 0.074 m in the control run. The Norwegian Sea side is unchanged. But the Wadden Sea level loses more tide: Helgoland 0.91 m (observed 1.06 m), Den Helder 0.48 m (0.64 m), Eemshaven 0.93 m (1.15 m), rms 0.29 m over its 53 gauges. Over all 370 gauges the rms is 0.157 m, against 0.149 m without the Wadden Sea level and 0.173 m in the control run. More water over the flats takes more tide out of the German Bight, and that is what brings the Skagerrak down. The Wadden Sea itself then needs less dissipation: less friction or more resolution in its channels, at 1.5 km now. That is the next test, together with the depths of the Norwegian Trench and the Skagerrak.

    A child's ring below its own bed on a drying flat

    solvedIDEAL2Ddrying

    The child's ring elevation is the parent's block mean (with limited slopes). On a sloping flat that mean lies below the bed of the higher child cells: in IDEAL's drying test the shelf's ring had water depths down to −0.06 m, and the shelf's own cells next to it drained below Dmin. Now the ring elevation is never below the child's bed + Dmin, and the parent's transports at the ring faces are scaled by pyGETM's drying factor from the child's own face depth (fork f274516). In deep water nothing changes.

    Parent faces open while the child's sub-faces are all closed

    solvedmasksNEABDKm

    The child-led land/sea consistency made a parent hole cell wet if any of its child cells was, and dried child ring cells over dry parent cells, but it left a parent interface face open when all its r child sub-faces were closed (the child's cells along it dry). Flux matching then set its transport to zero, but the parent's momentum equation kept one there. NEAB coarse had 12 such faces, NEAB fine 8, DKm 6. They are now closed in the parent's mask before its grids are built (fork b81ded2); a lake at rest stays at rest.

    pyGETM's 2D restart with open boundaries was not exact

    solvedpyGETMrestart

    A restart file holds the elevation after the open-boundary update at the end of the last step, but the next step's water depths are computed before that update, from the boundary elevations of the previous one. After a restart pyGETM computed them from the updated ones, and the run drifted (2.7e-7 m after 300 steps in a basin with one open boundary). 3D runs already restore the elevation from before the update (zin); 2D runs now take the boundary cells' elevation from zo, which holds exactly those values (fork a472d87). A single model now restarts bit-identically.

    A nested run restarts bit-identically

    solvedrestart

    A restarted nested run differed from an unbroken one: about 1e-6 m after two steps in NEAB (2D), and in IDEAL 3D 1e-5 °C after 6 h. Found and fixed (fork), until IDEAL 3D continued bit-identically after a restart in every field and level, with 1 and 2 processes per level and with 3D sub-stepping (November 2026):

    • The order of U and V alternates every step in pyGETM's momentum and was not in the restart file; now it is.
    • Open boundaries (pyGETM itself): at start-up pyGETM mirrored the U/V depths and layer thicknesses at open boundaries again, from other points than during the run. After a restart they are now kept.
    • Derived fields near the interfaces: friction, Coriolis, advection and the slow terms were computed from the transports before the nesting replaced them at the interface, but after a restart from the replaced ones. A new stage of the time step, before these diagnostics, now lets the nesting replace the transports first: they are computed once, at the same point of the step, from the transports actually used. This changes nested results slightly (rerun).
    • The summed 2D transports of a 3D step receive the parent's flux correction of the last micro step after their reset, so they are state; and the correction was applied by the subdomain owning the face only, leaving the neighbour's halo with the old value. They are now in the restart file, and their halos are updated once per 3D step. With that, results are also bit-identical between process splits.

    All fields use pyGETM's own restart file (fields marked as part of the state). Still to check: NEAB in 2D, and a restart file written by upstream pyGETM.
